The Shanepedia Cron System

The Shanepedia-specific cron stack as it stands on 2026-05-17. Five jobs, five different shapes, routed through OpenClaw and GPT-5.5. Together they keep the wiki alive in Shane's head without him having to open it, and they now include a quiet write-back loop for Shane's feedback and ideas.

The newest rule: the daily page cron must also leave a durable page behind. A useful Telegram nudge should become a wiki/daily/ page, show up in Shane Feed, and rebuild the public site.

This page documents what each one does, why it exists at the time and shape it does, and what would cause it to be killed.

1. Shanepedia Feedback Absorber

  • Schedule: every three hours during the active day.
  • Skill: inline cron prompt using the wiki and Shanepedia runtime contracts.
  • Model: openai-codex/gpt-5.5, light context.
  • Job to be done: read recent workspace memory, Shanepedia ideation state, and the live wiki; absorb concrete Shane feedback, preferences, and business ideas into the right existing page or ideation log; rebuild and lint after edits.
  • Why it exists: Shane often gives high-signal direction in short Telegram messages. The absorber is the safety net that turns those messages into durable wiki state instead of hoping a future chat can find them.
  • What makes it different: it is silent by default. The job is not to text Shane; the job is to update Shanepedia when there is something worth preserving.
  • Kill signal: it starts making speculative edits from weak signals. The correct behavior for ambiguous input is NO_CHANGE.

2. Shanepedia Daily Page

  • Schedule: 0 11 * * * — 11:00 daily, America/Los_Angeles.
  • Skill: ~/setup-mac/.agents/skills/shanepedia-daily-page/SKILL.md.
  • Model: openai-codex/gpt-5.5, light context, 300s timeout.
  • Job to be done: create or refresh one daily feed page, surface a wiki page or spark, pair it with one bounded Gemini-fetched YouTube find when available, rebuild/publish the public artifact, and ask Shane whether to add, update, productize, or ignore.
  • Why it exists: the wiki has ~100 pages. Without active surfacing, most of them go cold. The daily page rotation is the cheapest mechanism that keeps the corpus circulating in working memory.
  • What makes it different: the only Shanepedia cron that has a hard write-back contract: one wiki/daily/YYYY-MM-DD-shanepedia-feed.md page per day, linked from the feed archive.
  • Kill signal: Shane starts saying "ignore" to it three days in a row. Means either the random selection is wrong (fix the picker) or the wiki itself isn't producing anything worth surfacing (fix the wiki).

3. Shanepedia Random Spark

  • Schedule: 30 12 * * * — 12:30 daily, America/Los_Angeles.
  • Skill: runs inline from the cron prompt, calls scripts/wiki/random_spark_candidates.py with --include-youtube --gemini-timeout 18.
  • Model: openai-codex/gpt-5.5, light context, 150s timeout, no thinking.
  • Job to be done: broader cross-interest curiosity nudge. The candidate may be wiki-native or a public YouTube/Gemini-fetched item. One thing only, under 80 words, ends with one question (add / update / ignore).
  • Why it exists: the Daily Page is structured around the wiki as it currently is. The Random Spark is structured around what could enter the wiki next. It's the input pipeline counterpart to the Daily Page's maintenance pipeline.
  • What makes it different: explicitly grounded in the public web (Gemini + YouTube) rather than the wiki, while still routed through Shane's actual interests via the runtime summary. It's the most "outward facing" of the Shanepedia crons.
  • Kill signal: Gemini sparks stop landing as relevant — means the candidate-generation logic needs tightening, not that the slot is wrong.

4. Shanepedia One Bet

  • Schedule: 37 14 * * 2,4,6 — 14:37 Tue/Thu/Sat, America/Los_Angeles.
  • Skill: runs inline from the cron prompt, reads state/ideation/ideation-log.jsonl, state/ideation/preferences.jsonl, the roadmap, and the topic index.
  • Model: openai-codex/gpt-5.5, light context, 180s timeout.
  • Job to be done: propose one business bet — not three. Specific title, named buyer, why-now grounded in a wiki/recent signal, one proof step, one kill test. 140 words max. Must be materially different from the last 10 ideas.
  • Why it exists: Shane's portfolio of bets needs ongoing pressure-testing without becoming an idea-dump firehose. The three-times-weekly cadence is the frequency at which a new bet feels fresh but doesn't crowd out execution.
  • What makes it different: the only Shanepedia cron with a strict "buyer + proof step" contract. It refuses to deliver a bet that isn't testable. It's the wiki's mechanism for staying commercial, not just curatorial.
  • Kill signal: the bets repeat themselves or the proof steps stop being executable in a week. Means the recent-history filter is failing.

5. Shanepedia Weekly Roundup

  • Schedule: 0 19 * * 0 — Sunday 19:00, America/Los_Angeles.
  • Skill: ~/setup-mac/.agents/skills/shanepedia-weekly-roundup/SKILL.md.
  • Model: openai-codex/gpt-5.5, light context, 90s timeout.
  • Job to be done: Sunday-evening digest of what changed in the wiki that week, what's still open, one suggestion for next week.
  • Why it exists: the three daily/weekly slots above are about moves. The Roundup is about state. Without it, the daily slots can feel like a stream with no sense of accumulation. The Roundup makes the week visible as a unit.
  • What makes it different: the only Shanepedia cron that explicitly looks at log.md and reports on the wiki as a system, rather than asking for input. It's the closest thing to a chief-of-staff Sunday report.
  • Kill signal: the week genuinely had no signal worth reporting more than once or twice in a quarter. That would mean the wiki itself has stopped accumulating, which is a bigger problem than the cron slot.

How the four work together

Read as a set, the rhythm is:

  • Every three hours during the day — absorb concrete feedback and ideas.
  • Daily 11:00 — keep an existing page warm.
  • Daily 12:30 — surface a possibly-new thing from outside.
  • Tue/Thu/Sat 14:37 — pressure-test a commercial bet.
  • Sunday 19:00 — see the week as a whole.

That covers maintenance, ingest, commerce, and reflection. Each slot does one of those things and refuses to drift.

The slots also share a single delivery channel (Telegram, allowlist of one), a single voice contract (workspace/config/daily-update-voice.md), and a single failure-alert pattern (one Telegram alert per failure, 24h cooldown). They are intentionally homogeneous in shape so Shane only ever has to learn one set of conventions.

The rule

If any of these stop being useful, kill them. Crons should earn their slot.

enabled: false in jobs.json is the right move — don't delete. Disabled slots are a record of what was tried and what didn't work, and they are recoverable in one toggle if the use case comes back.
