Vending Machine Location Pitch — Finding and Winning Placements

Before you buy a machine, you need a location. Before you get a location, you need a pitch. This page covers how to find placement targets, what to say, what terms to propose, and the business structure behind the ask.

The fundamental trade you're making

You are offering a business owner:

  • A free amenity for their customers or staff
  • Passive revenue (commission on sales) with zero effort on their part
  • Zero cost to them — you pay for the machine, stock it, and maintain it

In exchange you get:

  • Floor or wall space (typically 4–8 sq ft)
  • Electricity (typically 100–200W, negligible cost)
  • Access to their foot traffic

This is a mutual benefit deal, not a sales pitch. Frame it that way.

Who to target first

Tier 1 — highest probability of yes

These venues have foot traffic, a captive audience, and a decision-maker who is easy to reach:

  • Auto repair shops: customers wait 30–90 minutes with nothing to do. Owner is usually on-site.
  • Laundromats: same dynamic. Long dwell time, bored customers.
  • Hair and nail salons: waiting area foot traffic, women-dominated clientele good for snack/drink machines.
  • Small gyms and martial arts studios: members want post-workout snacks; owner wants a member perk.
  • Tattoo parlors: long sessions, clients and friends waiting. Good cultural fit for a Bitcoin vending machine specifically.
  • Coffee shops and co-working spaces: Bitcoin-friendly demographics, ideal for a hot sauce or novelty machine.
  • Cannabis dispensaries (where legal): high dwell time, cash-heavy customers already comfortable with alternative payment.

Tier 2 — medium probability

  • Office buildings (facilities manager, not owner — harder to reach)
  • Hotels (regional manager approval often needed)
  • Apartment complexes (property management company)
  • Schools and colleges (requires facilities or admin approval; longer cycle)

Tier 3 — lowest probability for small operators

  • National chains (corporate approval, vendor credentialing required)
  • Hospitals (strict health code requirements)
  • Government buildings (procurement process)

Start with Tier 1. You can place three machines in auto repair shops while a single hotel approval is still in legal review.

Research before you walk in

Spend five minutes before approaching:

  1. Know the owner's name — Google, Yelp, LinkedIn, Facebook business page
  2. Know foot traffic roughly — busy street? parking lot full? note it
  3. Know what they already have — no machine = opportunity; bad machine = replacement opportunity
  4. Know what would sell there — snacks/drinks for a repair shop, healthy options for a gym, novelty/Bitcoin-themed for a tattoo parlor

You are not pitching a generic vending machine. You are pitching this machine, for these customers, at this location.

The in-person pitch (recommended)

In-person is always better than phone or email. You are asking for space in someone's business — they want to evaluate you as a person, not just a proposal.

Timing: Go mid-morning on a weekday (10am–noon). Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons.

Dress: Clean, not formal. Business casual or nice-casual. You are a small local operator, not a corporation. Authenticity matters more than a suit.

Opening line

"Hi, I'm [name]. I run a small vending operation in [city/neighborhood]. I was in the area and noticed you don't have a vending machine — I'd love to talk to you for two minutes about whether it might make sense here."

If they say they're busy:

"Totally understand. Can I leave you my card and come back at a better time?"

Core pitch (two minutes)

"I place and maintain [type] vending machines at local businesses. The way it works: I buy the machine, I stock it, I handle any maintenance. You don't pay anything. In return, I'd like to use [X sq ft] of your floor space near [waiting area / entrance / breakroom].

>

On the money side: I'd offer you [10–15%] of monthly revenue as a commission, paid monthly. At a modest [X] sales per day, that's roughly [$Y] per month for you — just for having the machine there. And your customers get a convenient option they didn't have before.

>

Would you be open to trying it for three months?"

If they ask about Bitcoin

For a Bitcoin Lightning vending machine, use this frame:

"The machine accepts both regular card payments and Bitcoin Lightning — it's a newer payment rail that's instant and has no chargebacks. I'm piloting this at a few locations. It's not a gimmick — it's just another way to pay, and customers who use it tend to be enthusiastic about it. It also makes for good word of mouth."

Do not over-explain Bitcoin. If they're curious, answer questions. If they're skeptical, acknowledge it and fall back to "card payments are the primary method anyway."

The phone pitch

Use this only as a follow-up after failing to reach the owner in person, or for Tier 2 locations.

"Hi, may I speak with the owner or manager?

>

[Pause]

>

Hi [name], my name is [name] — I'm a local vending operator. I'll keep this short. I place vending machines at businesses like yours at no cost to you — I handle everything. In exchange I pay a small commission on sales. I was curious if you'd have two minutes to hear more, or I could send a quick email with the details."

The email pitch

For cold outreach or follow-up after a call:

---

Subject: Free vending machine for [Business Name] — small commission offer

Hi [Name],

I'm [Name], a local vending operator based in [City]. I'm reaching out because I think [Business Name] could be a great fit for one of my machines.

Here's the short version:

  • I provide and maintain the vending machine at no cost to you
  • I stock it with [snacks / drinks / novelty items]
  • You earn [10–15%] of monthly sales as a commission — typically $[X]–[Y]/month for a location like yours
  • 3-month trial with no long-term commitment required

Your customers get a convenient option, you get passive income and an added amenity — no work on your end.

I'd love to swing by and take a look at your space. Would [Day] or [Day] work for a 10-minute visit?

Thanks, [Name] [Phone] [Website or Instagram if applicable]

---

The commission and contract structure

Typical commission rates

| Location desirability | Commission to host | |---|---| | Low traffic, off-street | 5–10% | | Moderate traffic, walk-in | 10–15% | | High traffic, captive audience | 15–25% |

Start at 10% for most locations. Go to 15% if they push back or the location is excellent. Going above 20% typically only makes sense for truly premium placement (busy airport lounge, large gym, etc.).

Fixed rent vs. commission

Some owners prefer a flat monthly fee over a percentage:

  • Flat fee pros: predictable for them, simple accounting
  • Flat fee cons: you absorb all downside risk if sales are low

Proposed flat fee ranges: $25–75/month for most small locations. Negotiate based on estimated sales volume.

The placement agreement (contract)

Always use a written agreement, even for a friend. Key sections:

  1. Parties: operator name, location owner name, business address
  2. Equipment description: machine make/model, serial number
  3. Placement location: specific spot within the business
  4. Term: start date, initial term (suggest 6–12 months), auto-renewal clause
  5. Termination: 30-day written notice by either party
  6. Commission: percentage of gross sales, payment schedule (monthly), payment method
  7. Operator responsibilities: stocking, maintenance, repairs within X days of reported issue
  8. Location owner responsibilities: provide electricity, reasonable access, notify operator of issues
  9. Liability: operator carries general liability insurance; machine is operator's property
  10. Disputes: state/county jurisdiction for any disputes

Free contract templates: VendSoft contract guide and GeniusVend template are good starting points.

Insurance

Before placing any machine, carry a general liability policy naming you as operator. Cost: $30–60/month for a small operator. Required by most serious locations. Provides protection if a machine malfunctions and causes injury or damage.

The Bitcoin angle as a differentiator

Most vending operators pitching the same location are offering the same commodity: snacks, Pepsi, cards-only.

A Bitcoin Lightning vending machine is a differentiator. Use it selectively:

  • Tattoo parlors, crypto-adjacent businesses: lead with it
  • Coffee shops, co-working spaces: mention it as a feature
  • Auto shops, laundromats: mention it only if they seem interested; card payments are primary
  • Conservative businesses (churches, schools): do not lead with Bitcoin; focus on card payments and reliability

For Bitcoin-friendly locations in Portland, check BTCMap.org — businesses already accepting Bitcoin are pre-qualified and will understand the pitch immediately.

Finding locations systematically

Ground game (most effective)

  • Walk or drive neighborhoods near your target area
  • Note every laundromat, auto shop, gym, salon, tattoo parlor, coffee shop
  • Build a spreadsheet: business name, address, owner name (if findable), existing machine (yes/no), visited (yes/no), outcome
  • Aim to approach 20 locations for every 1 placement you close (industry average is ~5–10%, higher with Bitcoin differentiator and good pitch)

Online research

  • Google Maps: search "[location type] near [city]" — check street view for waiting areas
  • Yelp: read reviews to understand the customer base
  • LinkedIn: find the decision-maker at offices and gyms
  • BTCMap.org: find Bitcoin-accepting businesses who are already warm to the idea

Referrals

After your first placement, ask the owner: "Do you know any other business owners in the area who might be interested?" A referral from a peer closes faster than any cold pitch.

What the business model actually looks like

Unit economics for one machine

| Item | Estimate | |---|---| | Machine cost (used, good condition) | $800–2,000 | | Monthly stocking cost | $150–300 | | Monthly commission to location | $30–75 | | Monthly maintenance / electricity | $20–40 | | Monthly gross revenue | $400–800 | | Monthly net (before machine payback) | $130–355 | | Machine payback period | 6–18 months |

These are rough estimates. A great location (busy auto shop, gym with 200+ members) can hit $800–1,200/month gross. A poor location is $150–250.

The 5-machine threshold

Most vending operators say the business becomes meaningfully profitable at 5+ machines because:

  • Fixed overhead (insurance, vehicle, tools) is spread across more revenue
  • Route efficiency improves — you can service 5 machines in a half day instead of separate trips
  • You have enough data to know which locations perform and which to exit

Bitcoin Lightning upside

If even 10–20% of transactions at a location go through Lightning:

  • You have zero chargebacks (irreversible)
  • You reduce card processing fees on that portion
  • You own the sats — they can appreciate
  • You become a node in the local circular economy

The Lightning revenue is not the main business model. The main model is snack/product sales. Lightning is the differentiator that gets you placements and press.
