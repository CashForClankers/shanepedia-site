Hot Sauce Vending Machine

A vending machine stocked with a friend's artisan hot sauce, placed at food cart pods, accepting Bitcoin Lightning and credit card. Low overhead, no staffing, bitcoin-native POS, local product distribution.

Why this matters now

This is the first concrete "owned spend surface" for the local bitcoin-commerce lane. It does three jobs at once:

  • creates a place where sats can actually be spent locally
  • proves out the Lightning + card payment stack on real hardware
  • gives Shane a physical product/business experiment that can expand into coffee, drinks, or other local goods later

The Core Concept

  • Product: Friend's hot sauce (consignment or wholesale split)
  • Placement: Food cart pods — high foot traffic, food-curious audience, no direct competition with the carts
  • Payments: Lightning Network (instant, ~1% fees) + credit card (Stripe Terminal or Square)
  • Ownership: Shane owns and operates the machine; friend gets per-bottle revenue share

Why Food Cart Pods Work

  • Captive audience already in a "spend money on interesting food" mindset
  • Pod operators often welcome complementary revenue — you're not competing with their carts
  • Portland has dozens of pods with different vibes; easy to test 1-2 before scaling
  • Low bureaucratic friction: placement on private property (pod owner permission only, no city permit needed for the machine itself)
  • Hot sauce is an impulse buy at $8–$15/bottle — perfect vending price point

Payment Stack Options

Option A: BTCPay Server + Lightning (Bitcoin-first)

  • Self-hosted BTCPay Server on a Raspberry Pi or VPS
  • Lightning invoice QR shown on screen; payment confirmed in seconds
  • Add Stripe Terminal (physical card reader) for credit card alongside it
  • Fees: Lightning ~0–1%, Stripe ~2.7% + $0.05
  • Best for: bitcoin-native branding, no middleman, full control
  • Reference: BTCPay Server POS — open source, free

Option B: OpenNode or Strike (hosted Lightning)

  • Managed Lightning without running your own node
  • Easier setup, slightly higher fees (~1%), less control
  • Good for MVP before committing to self-hosted stack

Option C: Square + Cash App (easiest, least bitcoin)

  • Square Terminal handles card + Cash App Pay (which supports Lightning via BOLT11)
  • Fastest path to deployed hardware
  • Tradeoff: no native Lightning wallet QR, Cash App intermediary

Bolt Card (NFC Lightning tap-to-pay)

  • Customers with a Bolt Card tap NFC to pay Lightning instantly
  • No QR scan needed — slick UX, especially for regulars
  • Worth supporting if going full bitcoin-native

Settled recommendation

After current-source review, the best first serious stack is:

  1. BTCPay Server for Lightning
  2. Stripe Terminal for cards
  3. Qibixx MDB Pi Hat Plus or MDB-USB for vending control

Recommended card-reader choice:

  • Best unattended path: Stripe Verifone UX700 if available, because Stripe positions it for self-service and recommends server-driven integration for it.
  • Fallback faster-to-start path: a more attended tablet + reader setup before moving to a fully self-service reader enclosure.

Why this wins over the earlier menu of options:

  • It keeps Lightning on an open, self-hosted stack.
  • It gives a realistic card path for normal customers instead of forcing Bitcoin-only behavior too early.
  • It avoids writing raw MDB protocol from scratch because Qibixx now exposes a higher-level interface.
  • It leaves room for future NFC/Bolt Card support without making that the Day 1 blocker.

Hardware Options

Off-the-Shelf Vending Machine

  • Standard snack/small-item vending machine ($800–$3,000 used, $2,000–$5,000 new)
  • Refrigerated not needed for shelf-stable hot sauce
  • Bolt on a tablet (iPad or Android) running BTCPay POS app + Stripe Terminal reader
  • Sources: eBay, Craigslist, vending machine liquidators

Custom / Smaller Format

  • Small countertop "smart locker" style (3–12 slots) — lower footprint, easier to negotiate placement
  • Companies: Adimac, or DIY with Raspberry Pi + servo locks + touchscreen
  • Better for testing before committing to full-size machine

The "Dude, Seriously" Precedent

  • Dude, Seriously already launched the world's first hot sauce vending machine at a food/drink venue in Cincinnati
  • Proves the concept works and people engage with it
  • Differentiation: go bitcoin-native + local craft product + food cart pod context

Business Model

| Revenue Source | Notes | |---|---| | Per-bottle margin | Buy from friend at wholesale (~$4–6), sell at $10–$14 | | OR consignment split | Friend gets 50–60%, you keep 40–50% per bottle | | Placement fee to pod | Typically $0 if you bring traffic; sometimes small monthly fee | | Bitcoin premium | Consider BTC-only price at $0.50 less — encourages Lightning adoption |

Unit economics example (wholesale model, 20 bottles/week):

  • Revenue: 20 × $12 = $240/week
  • COGS: 20 × $5 = $100
  • Payment fees (~2%): ~$5
  • Gross: ~$135/week per machine (~$7k/year)

Placement Strategy

  1. Start with 1 pod — negotiate a handshake deal with the pod operator (offer revenue share or flat $50–100/month)
  2. Target vibe: pods with a "foodie + adventurous" crowd, not just lunch workers
  3. Portland pods worth approaching: WonderLove PDX (SE Portland, elevated vibe), pods near Alberta Arts District, Mississippi Ave
  4. Pitch to operator: "I bring a unique attraction, zero staffing, and I handle restocking 1x/week"
  5. Scale: once one location is profitable and dialed in, clone to 2–3 more pods

Field Ops / "Rent-a-Human"

Even a low-staff machine still needs real-world operations. A lightweight human support layer can cover:

  • weekly restocking
  • jam checks and machine resets
  • card reader / screen troubleshooting
  • venue relationship maintenance
  • in-person merchant discovery while already out servicing the machine

That makes this lane a good fit for a "Rent-a-Human" style offer: paid field execution for local bitcoin-commerce infrastructure.

Bitcoin Angle / Branding Ideas

  • Machine wraps with "Pay with ⚡ Lightning" prominently — conversation starter
  • First purchase free with Lightning (loss leader for onboarding)
  • QR code on machine links to a simple "how to use Lightning" explainer
  • Name the machine something fun — "The Sauce Stack", "Hot Node", "Capsaicin ATM"
  • Partners naturally with CashForClankers bitcoin-curious audience

MVP Build Path

  1. Source a used snack vending machine with MDB support
  2. Set up BTCPay Server store + PoS / API flow for Lightning invoices
  3. Use Stripe Terminal for card acceptance
  4. Add Qibixx MDB interface so successful payment can trigger vend approval
  5. Load with friend's hot sauce
  6. Negotiate placement at 1 food cart pod
  7. Run for 60 days and track sales mix: Lightning vs card, top SKUs, failure modes, service burden
  8. Decide whether to:
  • keep scaling hot sauce
  • add coffee / drinks
  • or spawn a kid-friendly toy capsule variant

Open Questions

  • Does the friend want to be involved operationally, or pure product supply?
  • Refrigerated or room-temp storage? (most craft hot sauces are shelf-stable)
  • What's the restock cadence? (weekly is easy at food cart pod distance)
  • Machine ownership: buy used vs lease vs build custom?
  • LLC/insurance: needed before placing on someone else's property
