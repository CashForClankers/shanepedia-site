Kids activities and places

This page is the parent tracker for family-friendly places, activities, memberships, classes, pools, playgrounds, indoor energy burners, and seasonal ideas around Shane's Oregon home base in Portland / Gresham Home Base.

Purpose

The goal is not to build a giant directory of every kid-friendly venue in the Portland metro. The goal is to keep a practical shortlist of places that are actually useful for real family life with young kids:

  • easy wins for same-day kid outings when the morning falls apart and the original plan dies
  • reliable repeat options within a 25-minute drive of Gresham
  • weather-dependent backups for the long Pacific Northwest grey season (roughly October through May)
  • memberships or annual passes that pay off based on real attendance frequency, not aspirational use
  • notes about age fit, chaos level, parent recovery cost, and whether the place is genuinely worth the effort or just looks good on Instagram

The wiki should never pretend to be neutral about this. A page that lists every option without ranking is not useful when you have 90 minutes before naptime.

Why this matters for Shane

Family time is not a side project competing with the founder work — it is the gravity that anchors the whole Shane Neeley operating model. Most of Shane's other lanes (Bitcoin commerce research, vending machines, Shanepedia itself, the El Salvador trips) are deliberately structured so that the daily rhythm leaves room for kid time during the bulk of waking hours. That means the family-fun tracker is not a "soft" page in the wiki; it is operationally load-bearing. A bad Saturday morning costs the rest of the week.

It also means the bar for inclusion is high. A new venue earns a spot only after it has actually been used and the household agrees it was worth the drive. Aspirational additions get demoted to a separate "to try" list rather than polluting the trusted shortlist.

First lane: pools around Gresham

Pools are the inaugural concrete subtopic because they cover several use cases at once — energy burn, weather-proofing, lessons, and a built-in shower at the end. Capture options across:

  • Public pools: Mt. Hood Community College Aquatic Center, Gresham Aquatic at Sam Barlow, and the Multnomah County rec pools. Note hours, drop-in fees, family-swim windows, water temperature, and shallow-end depth.
  • Rec centers and YMCAs: Mt. Scott Community Center, East Portland Community Center, Mount Hood YMCA — membership math vs. drop-in for a family of four.
  • Hotel and gym pool day passes: less obvious but useful for mid-week emergencies and birthday-party logistics.
  • Swim lesson venues: Goldfish Swim School, British Swim School, plus the rec-center group lessons. Track teacher consistency and trial-class availability.
  • Splash pads and shallow-water options: Gresham Main City Park splash pad, Salish Ponds, summer-only sprinklers in neighborhood parks. These are the highest-leverage option in July and August for low-prep outings.

What to capture per place

Standard fields per entry, so the list is searchable and rank-able:

  • place name and address
  • area and drive time from Gresham (in actual minutes, not Google's optimistic guess)
  • public vs. private vs. membership
  • pool type or activity type (lap, warm-water, shallow play, slides, splash pad; or for non-pool entries: indoor play, outdoor playground, class, museum)
  • kid age fit (baby, toddler, preschool, early elementary, older)
  • price and membership model (drop-in vs. punch card vs. annual)
  • chaos level (calm, moderate, loud) — affects parent recovery cost
  • hours and busy windows (especially after-school and weekend mornings)
  • what makes it especially good or especially annoying — be specific
  • last visited date, so stale entries get re-verified

Adjacent lanes worth growing

Pools are the entry point, but the same shortlist discipline should apply to:

  • indoor play options for grey-season Saturdays (trampoline parks, indoor climbing for kids, OMSI, museum memberships)
  • playground winners ranked by age and weather (covered structures matter October–April)
  • kid sports, classes, and lessons (martial arts, soccer, gymnastics, music)
  • summer-friendly water spots (Sandy River accessible spots, Sellwood pool, splash pads)
  • one-off seasonal ideas (pumpkin patches, U-pick berries, snow-play day trips up Highway 26)
  • backyard-resident options at home — the Backyard Chickens — Oregon Keeper Notes coop and routine egg collection is, functionally, a daily kids activity that costs nothing per use

Operating rule

Bias toward real usability over completeness. A smaller trusted list of places that have actually worked is more valuable than a giant stale directory that includes a dozen venues someone read about once. When in doubt, demote rather than include. The Saturday-morning use case is unforgiving: if Shane is scrolling this page at 9:15am with a four-year-old asking what's happening, the page has to deliver one good answer in under 30 seconds, not a comprehensive catalogue.
